## Plain-English summary

Omega-3 fatty acids appear constantly in joint-health marketing for dogs. The underlying clinical question — whether and how omega-3 helps dogs with osteoarthritis — is studied, but product claims often outrun what a careful reading supports for *your* dog. PAWai’s Knowledge Engine marked topic RT-001 (omega-3 for canine osteoarthritis) as completed in the research queue. This page is an **evidence-aware education card**, not a treatment protocol. Note: a dedicated full-text knowledge_dataset row for the primary RCT was not reliably persisted in an earlier pipeline save bug, so we keep claims intentionally conservative here.

## How to use this without overreaching

- Treat social “miracle joint” claims as marketing until a vet says otherwise
- Bring a mobility timeline to the clinic (when stiffness shows up, what makes it better/worse)
- Ask whether imaging, pain scoring, weight management, or pharmacologic options are appropriate *before* stacking supplements

## Red flags — see a vet promptly

- Sudden non-weight-bearing lameness
- Severe pain, fever, or rapid worsening
- Collapse or inability to rise

These are not “supplement problems.” They need veterinary assessment.

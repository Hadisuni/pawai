## Plain-English summary

If your dog is being treated for itching, someone will eventually ask whether it is better. That question is harder than it sounds — you are comparing today against a memory of several weeks ago, which is exactly the kind of comparison people are worst at.

This 2019 study tested a small intervention: let the owner see the number they gave last time, before they give a new one. Agreement between the score change and the owner's own sense of whether the dog had improved rose from 79% to 96%.

The finding is genuinely interesting for anyone who keeps a health record — and it comes with a caveat sharp enough that it deserves stating alongside the number rather than after it.

## What the study found

One hundred and ninety-two pruritic dogs were enrolled prospectively, with a further 196 examined retrospectively. Owners were randomly assigned to five groups, and Pruritus Visual Analog Scale scores were recorded across two consecutive visits.

The groups differed in how the scale was presented. Group A saw their previous scores before completing the scale. Group B completed it, were then shown their previous scores, and asked to repeat it. Group C completed it as normally reported, with no reference to previous scores. Group D completed the visual scale plus a 0–10 verbal scale.

Agreement with the owner's perception of improvement varied sharply by method. Group A reached 96% average agreement with a kappa of 0.81. Group B reached 80% before seeing previous scores and 82% after. Group D reached 85% (kappa 0.47). Group C — the standard approach, with no previous score shown — was lowest at 79% and a kappa of 0.37.

In Group D, the visual and verbal scales did not differ significantly from each other (P = 0.56).

The retrospective analysis, looking across at least three consecutive visits, found much weaker agreement overall: 50.8% average and a kappa of 0.25. The best values, 63% and 0.355, occurred at visit intervals of 30 to 60 days.

The authors' conclusion is modest and specific: showing owners previous scores could improve how the scale captures the owner's perception of their dog's itching level.

## What this study cannot tell you

This is the important part, and it runs against the conclusion a record-keeping product would like to draw.

The study measured agreement between the change in score and the owner's own reported perception of improvement. It did not measure whether either of those corresponds to the dog's actual itch. An owner who sees they wrote 7 last time and believes the treatment is working may write 4 this time partly because that is the shape of the story — anchoring, not observation. This design cannot separate a genuinely better assessment from a more internally consistent one.

So "seeing the previous score improves the record" is supported. "Seeing the previous score makes owners more accurate about their dog" is not, and should not be claimed.

The retrospective agreement of 50.8% across real consecutive visits is also a sobering number. In ordinary practice, across ordinary intervals, owner scoring and owner perception line up about half the time.

Finally, this is a single-centre study of dogs already under care for itching, using one scale. It says nothing about detecting skin disease, and nothing about what any particular score means clinically.

## What pet owners should track

If your dog is under treatment for itching, the useful record is short and dated:

- The score your veterinarian's tool produces, kept with its date so you can see it at the next visit
- Where on the body your dog is scratching, licking or chewing, and whether that has moved
- Whether the itching is worse at particular times of day or year
- Any change in treatment, with the date it started — this is the anchor everything else is read against
- Aim to review roughly monthly if your vet agrees; agreement in this study was best at 30–60 day intervals

Bring the previous number with you. On this evidence, having it in front of you changes how you answer.

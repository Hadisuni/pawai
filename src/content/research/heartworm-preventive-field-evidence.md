## Plain-English summary

Heartworm is transmitted by mosquitoes and can cause life-threatening disease in dogs. Prevention is far safer than treating an established infection — but *which* preventive, *when* to start, and *how* to test are clinical decisions. A 2026 paper in *Parasites & Vectors* reported a multicenter field trial of a novel chewable tablet (Credelio Quattro) aimed at preventing *Dirofilaria immitis* infection in dogs. PAWai surfaces studies like this so you arrive at the clinic with better questions, not so you can self-prescribe.

## What this study is useful for

- Understanding that modern preventives are studied in formal field programs, not only lab models
- Reminding owners that “viral scare posts” are not a substitute for local veterinary guidance
- Prompting a conversation about testing cadence and travel/mosquito exposure

## What this study is *not*

- Not dosing advice
- Not proof that one brand is right for every dog
- Not a reason to skip veterinary exams or antigen testing when your vet recommends them

## What to track before your next vet visit

- Whether your dog is currently on a preventive (name, last dose date if known)
- Recent travel to higher-risk regions
- Any missed doses or gaps you remember
- Cough, exercise intolerance, or other respiratory/energy changes — mention them; do not self-diagnose heartworm from a post

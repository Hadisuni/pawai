## The problem that's easy to shrug off

Your dog shakes their head after a nap, rubs one side of their face along the sofa, or scratches at an ear until it's pink. Maybe there's a sour smell when you lean in for a cuddle. It doesn't look serious, so it's tempting to wait and see, or to reach for the leftover drops from last time.

Ear trouble is one of the most common reasons dogs see a vet, and also one of the most commonly brushed aside. A short written history of each episode is one of the most useful things you can bring to the appointment.

## What the research found

A 2021 study in *Canine Medicine and Genetics* used VetCompass, a project that collects anonymised records from UK primary-care vet clinics. It looked at a random sample of **22,333 dogs** seen during 2016.

- **7.30%** were diagnosed with otitis externa, which is inflammation of the outer ear canal, within that single year. That's roughly **one dog in fourteen**.
- **Ear shape mattered.** Compared with breeds that have upright ears, breeds with hanging (pendulous) ears had **1.76 times** the odds, and breeds with V-shaped drop ears **1.84 times** the odds.
- **Breed mattered most.** The Basset Hound had **5.87 times** the odds of a crossbred dog, followed by the Chinese Shar Pei (3.44), Labradoodle (2.95), Beagle (2.54), and Golden Retriever (2.23).
- Some breeds had **lower** odds than crossbreds, including the Chihuahua, Border Collie, Yorkshire Terrier, and Jack Russell Terrier.

A separate 2023 review of dogs with repeated ear infections adds a point every owner of a "sore ears" dog should hear: **recurrent ear infections are always secondary to something else**, such as an allergy or another underlying condition. Treating each flare with another course of drops doesn't fix that cause. The review argues that most ear surgery could be avoided if recurring cases were worked up properly earlier.

## What it can't tell you

The 7.30% only counts dogs whose ear problem was recorded at a vet visit. Dogs that were never brought in, or were treated at home, aren't in the number, so the real figure is likely higher.

Ear shape was assigned by breed, not measured on each dog. Within a breed, individual ears vary, so this study can't say anything about your particular dog's risk. Owners of breeds known for ear trouble may also bring them in sooner, which can make those breeds look more affected.

The study didn't test any prevention or treatment. Nothing here shows that cleaning, drying, plucking hair, or changing diet works. The authors specifically warn against overzealous ear cleaning and favour cautious, low-harm advice from your vet.

## Signs that can't wait

### Call a vet or emergency clinic now

- A **sudden head tilt**, loss of balance, circling, falling to one side, or eyes flicking back and forth
- **Severe pain**: crying out, or not letting anyone near the head
- A **hot, swollen, puffy ear flap**, which can happen suddenly after hard shaking
- **Facial drooping**, or your dog can't blink or close the eye on one side
- **Lethargy or not eating** together with ear signs, or any sign of injury to the ear
- A **grass seed or other object** you think may have gone into the ear, especially after a walk through long grass

### Same-day or next-day vet

- Constant **head shaking or scratching** at one ear
- **Discharge, bleeding, or a strong new smell**
- A red, swollen, or narrowed ear opening
- Your dog suddenly **won't let you touch their head** or ears

### Worth booking an appointment

- Mild itch or smell that **keeps coming back**
- A dog on their **third or fourth course of ear drops** in a year or two
- Ear trouble that **appears with itchy paws or skin**, or at the same time every year

Please don't put anything into your dog's ear canal, including old drops, home remedies, or cotton buds, unless your vet has told you to. If the eardrum is damaged, some products can make things worse, and only an exam can check that.

## What to keep in your dog's record

- **The date each episode started**, and when it fully cleared
- **Which ear**: left, right, or both. One-sided problems can have different causes from two-sided ones
- **What you notice**: head shaking, scratching, smell, discharge colour, redness
- **What the vet found and prescribed**, and whether it worked completely or the problem came straight back
- **Other itch**, such as licking paws, scratching the belly, or face rubbing, since ear problems often travel with skin allergies
- **Swimming, baths, or grooming** in the days before a flare
- **A clear photo** of the ear opening, taken in good light, for comparison over time

## How PAWai fits in

PAWai can't look inside an ear, doesn't diagnose ear disease, and doesn't advise on cleaning, products, or treatment. Ear problems need an otoscope exam, and often a swab looked at under a microscope, to see what's really going on. What PAWai can do is keep every episode, side, treatment, and outcome on one dated timeline, so "he gets ear trouble sometimes" becomes a pattern your vet can act on.

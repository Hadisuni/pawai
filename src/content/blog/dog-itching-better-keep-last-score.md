## The question that's harder than it sounds

Your dog has been scratching, licking their paws, or chewing at their side, and you've started treatment with your vet. A few weeks later, at the follow-up, someone asks: "So, is the itching better?"

You want to give a good answer. But you're comparing today with a memory of how things were a month or more ago, and that's exactly the kind of comparison people find hardest. Did they scratch less last night, or did you just sleep through it?

## What the research found

A 2019 study in *Veterinary Dermatology* looked at how owners use the Pruritus Visual Analog Scale, a tool vets use to rate how itchy a dog is. **192 itchy dogs** were enrolled, and owners were randomly assigned to groups that were shown the scale in different ways across two consecutive visits.

- Owners who **saw their previous score before rating again** had their score change agree with their own sense of whether the dog had improved **96%** of the time.
- Owners who rated **the usual way, with no previous score in front of them**, agreed only **79%** of the time, the lowest of the groups.
- In the group that used both the visual scale and a simple **0–10 number**, the two didn't differ significantly. A separate 2022 study found the 0–10 scale valid, but not interchangeable with the visual one, so it's best to stay with whichever tool your clinic uses.

A second, retrospective part of the study looked at records from **196 further dogs** across at least three visits in ordinary practice. There, score changes and owners' sense of improvement lined up only **50.8%** of the time on average. The best agreement came when visits were **30 to 60 days apart**.

## What it can't tell you

This is the important part. The study measured whether the score change matched the owner's own feeling of improvement. It didn't measure whether either matched how itchy the dog actually was.

An owner who sees they wrote 7 last time, and believes the treatment is working, may write 4 this time partly because that fits the story. So seeing the previous score makes the record more consistent. It doesn't prove owners become more accurate about their dog.

It was also a single-centre study of dogs already being treated for itch, using one scale. It says nothing about finding the cause of itching, and nothing about what any particular score means. Itch has many causes, and only a vet can work out which one applies.

## Signs that can't wait

### Call a vet or emergency clinic now

- **Sudden swelling of the face**, muzzle, or around the eyes, or hives appearing quickly
- **Trouble breathing**, collapse, or repeated vomiting alongside sudden itching or swelling
- A patch of skin that is **hot, very painful, or spreading fast**, especially if it's oozing or bleeding
- **Wounds your dog has made by scratching** that are deep, bleeding, or won't stop being chewed
- Ear scratching together with a **head tilt, loss of balance, or unusual eye movements**
- Your dog seems **unwell in themselves**: lethargic, off their food, or feverish, as well as itchy

### Same-day or next-day vet

- Itching that is **suddenly much worse** or keeping your dog awake all night
- **Red, raw, or smelly skin**, or hair loss that's appearing quickly
- **Ears that are red, smelly, or painful** to touch
- Itching that **started or got worse after a new treatment**

### Worth booking an appointment

- Itching that comes back **at the same time each year** or keeps returning
- Constant **paw licking** or chewing that has gone on for weeks
- You're on treatment and **genuinely can't tell** whether it's working

Please don't try to work out the cause at home or swap treatments on your own. A score is only useful once a vet knows what's causing the itch.

## What to keep in your dog's record

- **The score from your vet's tool**, with its date, so you have it in front of you at the next visit
- **Where your dog scratches, licks, or chews**, and whether that has moved
- **When it's worse**: time of day, after walks, or time of year
- **Any change in treatment, with the start date**. Everything else is read against this
- **A rough monthly review** if your vet agrees, since agreement in the study was best at 30 to 60 days

Only score at home if your vet asks you to, and use the tool they give you. Then bring the previous number with you.
